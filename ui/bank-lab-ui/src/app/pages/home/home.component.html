The mental model

Everything in this lab hangs off one picture. If you remember nothing else, remember the two tellers.

Concurrency

one teller, many customers

The teller serves you, and while you fill in a form they turn to the next customer. Nobody is served simultaneously — there is one teller — but five customers are in progress at once.

One thread, many operations in flight. That is async/await. The thread is not watching you write — it goes elsewhere and comes back.

Parallelism

many tellers, many counters

Four tellers at four counters. Four customers genuinely served at the same instant. You cannot get this by reorganising one person’s time — you need more tellers.

}

Multiple cores, all busy. That is Parallel.ForEach and PLINQ. A core is a teller.

async is not parallel

And Task.Run is not async — it moves blocking work to another thread and waits for it. Measured at 1,857× slower than just calling the method.

A race is a gap

Not “two things at once”. It is read → think → write where somebody wrote during your think. One core, one thread, still races.

Optimistic vs pessimistic

Collisions rare → detect and retry. Collisions common → queue up and wait. The deciding variable is the conflict rate, not preference.
